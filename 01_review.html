Review

The Wrong Memory addresses the familiar subject of C memory-safety vulnerabilities, but approaches it through a broader conceptual framework. Its individual themes—including vulnerability analysis, semantic invariants, memory-safe language design, and the contrast between C and Rust—are well established. What stands out in the book is the way these themes are connected.

It begins by asking what goes wrong in C, then examines the relationships and assumptions that repeatedly fail. These are developed through the idea of semantic invariants, leading to a larger question: what would a programming language need to represent or enforce those invariants? From there, the book considers whether C itself can provide stronger guarantees without ceasing to be C, and finally examines what changes when a language such as Rust makes more of those relationships explicit and enforceable.

The result is an attempt to connect vulnerability analysis, programming-language design, and the programmer's mental model of memory safety. Whether that framework is useful will depend on the reader, but the connections between these subjects form the book's central contribution.
